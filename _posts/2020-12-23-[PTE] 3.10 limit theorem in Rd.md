## Definitions
<div class="definition" text="distribution function">
$F:\mathbb{R}^d\to\mathbb{R}$ is a distribution function if it is<br>
(i) non-decreasing.<br>
(ii) right-continuous.<br>
(iii) $\lim_{x\to-\infty}F(x)=0$ and $\lim_{x\to\infty}F(x)=1.$ Where $x\to\infty$ means $x_i\to\infty$ for all $i=1,\cdots,d$ and $x\downarrow x$ means $y_i\downarrow x_i$ for all $i.$ </br>
(iv) $\Delta_A F := P(X\in A) \ge 0$ for all rectangle $A,$ where $P(X\in A) = \sum_{v\in V}\text{sgn}(v)F(v),$ $A=(a_1,b_1]\times\cdots\times(a_d,b_d],$ $V=\{a_1,b_1\}\times\cdots\times\{a_d,b_d\},$ $\text{sgn}(v) = (-1)^{(\text{\# of } a_i \text{'s in } v)}.$
</div><br>


## Limit theorems
<div class="definition" text="Lipschitz continuity">
$f$ is Lipschitz continuous on a metric space $(S,\rho)$ if there exists $c\in\mathbb{R}$ such that $|f(x)-f(y)|\le c\cdot\rho(x,y)$ for all $x,y\in S.$
</div>

We arrive at another portmanteau theorem.

<div class="theorem" text="Portmanteau theorem">
The followings are equivalent.<br>
(i) $Eg(X_n) \to Eg(X_\infty),~ \forall g:$ bounded, continuous. <br>
(ii) $Eg(X_n) \to Eg(X_\infty),~ \forall g:$ bounded, Lipschitz continuous. <br>
(iii) $\limsup_n P(X_n \in F) \le P(X_\infty \in F),~ \forall F:$ closed. <br>
(iv) $\liminf_n P(X_n \in G) \ge P(X_\infty \in G),~ \forall G:$ open. <br>
(v) $\lim_n P(X_n\in A) = P(X_\infty \in A),~ \forall A:~ P(X_\infty \in \partial A)=0.$ <br>
(vi) $Ef(X_n) \to Ef(X_\infty),~ \forall f:$ bounded and $P(X_\infty \in D_f) = 0.$ <br>
</div><br>

In addition, we get multi-dimensional version of tightness theorem.

<div class="definition" text="uniform tightness">
$(\mu_n)$ is uniformly tight if for all $\epsilon > 0,$ there exists $M$ such that $\liminf_n \mu_n([-M,M]^d) \ge 1-\epsilon.$
</div>

<div class="theorem" text="Prohorov">
If $(\mu_n)$ is tight, there exists a weakly convergent subsequence.
</div>

The proof uses both Helly's selection theorem and uniform tightness.<br><br>


## Characteristic functions and inversion
<div class="definition" text="characteristic function">
$$\varphi(\mathbf{t}) := Ee^{i\mathbf{t}\cdot\mathbf{X}} = Ee^{i(t_1X_2+\cdots+t_dX_d)}$$ where $\mathbf{t}=(t_1,\cdots,t_d)$ and $\mathbf{X}=(X_1,\cdots,X_d).$
</div>

<div class="theorem" text="inversion formula">
Let $A=[a_1,b_1]\times\cdots\times[a_d,b_d]$ such that $\mu(\partial A)=0.$ Let $\varphi$ be a ch.f. of $\mu.$ Define 
$$\psi_j(t_j) = \frac{1}{it_j}\left( e^{-it_ja_j} - e^{-it_jb_j} \right)$$
then
$$\mu(A) = \lim_T \frac{1}{(2\pi)^d} \int_{[-T,T]^d} \prod_{j=1}^d \psi_j(t_j) \varphi(\mathbf{t})d\mathbf{t}.$$
</div>

We achieve the result by Fubini's theorem and the inversion formula for random variables.<br><br>


## Central limit theorem in $\mathbb{R}^d$

<div class="theorem" text="Levy's convergence">
Let $\mathbf{X}_n$,  $1\le n\le\infty$ be random vectors with ch.f. $\varphi_n.$ then $\mathbf{X}_n \overset{w}{\to} \mathbf{X}_\infty$ if and only if $\varphi_n(\mathbf{t}) \to \varphi_\infty(\mathbf{t}).$
</div>

<div class="corollary" text="Cramer-Wold device">
$$\mathbf{\theta}\cdot\mathbf{X}_n \overset{w}{\to} \mathbf{\theta}\cdot\mathbf{X}_\infty,~ \forall \mathbf{\theta} \in \mathbb{R}^d \implies \mathbf{X}_n \overset{w}{\to} \mathbf{X}_\infty.$$
</div>

Cramer-Wold device implies if every linear combination converges weakly to the same random variable, then the random vector itself weakly converges. In fact for normal random vector we can prove that $\mathbf{X} \sim \mathcal{N}_d(0,\mathbf{\Gamma})$ if and only if $\mathbf{t}\cdot\mathbf{X} \sim \mathcal{N}(0,\mathbf{t}'\mathbf{\Gamma}\mathbf{t})$ for all $\mathbf{t} \in \mathbb{R}^d.$

<div class="theorem" text="central limit theorem in $\mathbb{R}^d$">
$\mathbf{X}_i$'s are i.i.d. random vectors with $E\mathbf{X}_i=\mathbf{\mu},$ $\Gamma_{ij} = E(X_{ni}-\mu_i)(X_{nj}-\mu_j).$ Let $\mathbf{S}_n = \mathbf{X}_1+\cdots+\mathbf{X}_n$, then $\frac{\mathbf{S}_n - n\mathbf{\mu}}{\sqrt n} \overset{w}{\to} \mathcal{N}_d(\mathbf{0}, \mathbf{\Gamma}).$
</div>
<div class="proof"><br>
Without loss of generality, let $\mathbf{\mu}=0.$ Given $\mathbf{t} \in \mathbb{R}^d,$
$$\frac{1}{\sqrt n} \mathbf{t}\cdot\mathbf{S}_n = \frac{1}{\sqrt n} \sum\limits_{i=1}^n \mathbf{t}\cdot\mathbf{X}_i \overset{w}{\to} \mathcal{N}(0,\sigma_t^2)$$
where $\sigma_t^2 = \text{Var}(\mathbf{t}\cdot\mathbf{X}_i) = \mathbf{t}'\mathbf{\Gamma}\mathbf{t}.$ By Cramer-Wold device and the fact, the desired result follows.
</div>
