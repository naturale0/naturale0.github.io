Let our model be $y = X\beta + \epsilon$. Here, the design matrix $X$ is an augmented one. Our objective is to estimate the regression coefficient $\beta$ with $\hat{\beta}$.

By the least-square or the maximum likelihood, $\hat{\beta} = (X'X)^{-1}X'y$ is the estimate. what happens if we centered the data before fitting the model? will the values of coefficients change? (Spoiler alert: the title)

### 1. centering the predictors
Since the $\mathbb{1}$ column vector inside the design matrix is not affected by centering, we will split $X$ into $\mathbb{1}$ and the matrix of observed predictor values, then center the values in the latter.

$$ X_c = \begin{bmatrix} 
        \mathbb{1} & X_1
        \end{bmatrix} $$ 

is the centered and augmented design matrix.

Our regression coefficients for the centered form is specified as

$$
\beta_c = \begin{bmatrix} 
            \alpha \\
            \beta_1 \\
            \vdots \\
            \beta_p
          \end{bmatrix}
        = \begin{bmatrix}
            \alpha \\
            \boldsymbol{\beta_1}
          \end{bmatrix}
$$ 

where

$$
\alpha = \beta_0 + \beta_1 \bar{x_1} + \cdots + \beta_p \bar{x_p}, \:\:
\boldsymbol{\beta_1} = \begin{bmatrix}
                     \beta_1 \\
                     \vdots \\
                     \beta_p
                   \end{bmatrix}.
$$


Then our model becomes 

$$\begin{align}
y &= X_c \beta_c + \epsilon \\
  &= \alpha \cdot \mathbb{1} + X_1 \boldsymbol{\beta}_1 + \epsilon .
\end{align}$$

Using this to fit the model yields

$$ \hat{\beta_c} = (X_c'X_c)^{-1}X_c'y $$

and this values are the same to the ones from the uncentered one **except** the intercept ($\hat{\beta_0}$ from the original model, $\hat{\alpha}$ from the centered one).
