I would like to finish reviewing *Probability theory I* by briefly mentioning the *Poisson convergence* (section 3.6) and *limit theorems in $\mathbb{R}^d$* (3.10).

Poisson convergence is about limiting laws of a sequence of independent discrete Bernoulli-like random variables with $ES_n$ converges to a constant $\lambda.$ I used the term "Bernoulli-like" because for large enough $n,$ such $X_n$ should behave similar to Bernoulli random variables. For those sequences, the limiting law is not normal, but actually Poisson.

## Basic Poisson convergence
<div class="theorem" text="3.6.1">
Let $(X_{nk})_{k=1}^n$ be a rowwise independent triangular array with $P(X_{nk}=1)=p_{nk}$ and $P(X_{nk}=0)=1-p_{nk}.$ As $n\to\infty,$ if <br>
(i) $\sum_{k=1}^n p_{nk} \to \lambda \in (0,\infty).$<br>
(ii) $\max_{1\le k\le n} p_{nk} \to 0.$<br>
Then $S_n=\sum_{k=1}^n X_{nk} \overset{w}{\to} \mathcal{P}(\lambda).$
</div>
<details>
  <summary>
    expand proof
  </summary>

<div class="proof"><br>
As in the proof of the CLT, it suffices to show $\varphi_{S_n}(t) \to \exp(\lambda(e^{it}-1)).$
$$\begin{aligned}
\varphi_{S_n}(t)
  &= \prod_{k=1}^n \varphi_{nk}(t) \\
  &= \prod_{k=1}^n \left( p_{nk}(e^{it}-1) + 1 \right).
\end{aligned}$$
Thus
$$\begin{aligned}
&\left| \varphi_{S_n} - e^{\lambda(e^{it}-1)} \right| \\
&= \left| \prod_{k=1}^n \left( p_{nk}(e^{it}-1) + 1 \right) - \prod_{k=1}^n e^{p_{nk}(e^{it}-1)} \right| \\
&= \sum_{k=1}^n \left| p_{nk}(e^{it}-1)+1 - e^{p_{nk}(e^{it}-1)} \right| \\
&\;\;\;\;(b = p_{nk}(e^{it}-1),~ |b| \le 2p_{nk} \le 1 \text{ for large n.}) \\
&\le \sum_{k=1}^n p_{nk}^2 |e^{it}-1|^2 
 \le 4 \sum_{k=1}^n p_{nk}^2 \\
&\le 4 \max_{1\le k\le n} p_{nk} \sum_{k=1}^n p_{nk} \to 0.
\end{aligned}$$
The first inequality is from the lemmas we covered before
$$\begin{aligned}
&|p_{nk}(e^{it}-1)+1| \le 1-p_{nk}+p_{nk}|e^{it}| \le 1.\\
&|e^{p_{nk}(e^{it}-1)}| \le e^{p_{nk} |e^{it}-1|} \le e^{2p_{nk}} \le 1 \text{ for large } n.
\end{aligned}$$
</div></details>

For a special case, consider $X_k \overset{\text{iid}}{\sim} \mathcal{B}(p_n)$ for $k=1,\cdots,n$ where $np_n\to\mu.$ Then $S_n \overset{w}{\to} \mathcal{P}(\mu).$<br><br>


## General Poisson convergence
<div class="theorem" text="Poisson convergence">
Let $(X_{nk})_{k=1}^n$ be rowwise independent, non-negative, integer-valued triangular array with $P(X_{nk}=1) = p_{nk}$ and $P(X_{nk} \ge 2) = \epsilon_{nk}.$ As $n\to\infty$ if <br>
 (i) $\sum_{k=1}^n p_{nk} \to \lambda \in (0,\infty).$<br>
 (ii) $\max_{1\le k\le n} p_{nk} \to 0.$<br>
 (iii) $\sum_{k=1}^n \epsilon_{nk} \to 0.$<br>
then $S_n = \sum_{k=1}^n X_{nk} \overset{w}{\to} \mathcal{P}(\lambda).$
</div>
<div class="proof"><br>
Let $X_{nk}' = X_{nk}$ if $X_{nk}=1$ and $0$ otherwise. Then
$$\begin{aligned}
P(S_n \ne S_n') \le \sum_{k=1}^n P(X_{nk} \ge 2) = \sum_{k=1}^n \epsilon_{nk} \to 0.
\end{aligned}$$
By basic Poisson convergence theorem, $S_n' \overset{w}{\to} \mathcal{P}(\lambda).$ By the <a href="https://naturale0.github.io/probability/PTE-3.2.1-weak-convergence#converging-together-lemmas">converging together lemma</a>, we get the desired result.
</div><br>


## Total variation and weak convergence
Lastly, I will introduce *total variation*, a metric on a space of discrete probability measures. Like Levy metric, convergence in total variation distance is equivalent to weak convergence on such space.

<div class="definition" text="total variation">
Let $\mu,\nu$ be measures on countable space $\mathcal{S}.$<br>
$$\begin{aligned}
\|\mu-\nu\| 
 &:= \frac{1}{2} \sum_{z\in\mathcal{S}} |\mu(z) - \nu(z)| \\
 &= \sup_{A\subset\mathcal{S}} |\mu(A) - \nu(A)|
\end{aligned}$$
is the total variation distance between $\mu$ and $\nu.$
</div>

While the first formula is the definition, the second one is a derived property. Note that

$$\begin{aligned}
\sum_{z\in\mathcal{S}}|\mu(z)-\nu(z)| 
 &\ge |\mu(A) - \nu(A)| + |\mu(A^c)-\nu(A^c)| \\
 &= 2|\mu(A)-\nu(A)|.
\end{aligned}$$

Let $A=\\{z:~ \mu(z) \ge \nu(z)\\}$ then the equality holds.

<div class="lemma" text="total variation and weak convergence">
(i) $d(\mu, \nu) = \|\mu-\nu\|$ is a metric on the space of probability measures on $\mathbb{Z}.$ <br>
(ii) $\|\mu_n-\mu\|\to0$ if and only if $\mu_n(z)\to\mu(z)$ for all $z\in\mathbb{Z}.$ (i.e. $\mu_n \overset{w}{\to} \mu.$)
</div>
<div class="proof"><br>
(ii)<br>
($\Rightarrow$) Given $z\in\mathbb{Z},$
$$\begin{aligned}
|\mu_n(z)-\mu(z)| \le \sup_{A\subset\mathbb{Z}}|\mu_n(A)-\mu(A)| \to 0.
\end{aligned}$$
($\Leftarrow$) $\mu_n \overset{w}{\to} \mu$ then 
$$\begin{aligned}
\frac{1}{2}\sum_{z\in\mathbb{Z}} |\mu_n(z)-\mu(z)|
 = \sum_{z\in\mathbb{Z}} (\mu_(z)-\mu(z))^+ \to 0
\end{aligned}$$ by DCT.
</div>
