## Biography

I am Sihyung Park (pronounced "See-He-Young"), a Ph.D. candidate in the Department of Statistics at North Carolina State University, co-advised by Professors [Wenbin Lu](https://www4.stat.ncsu.edu/~wlu4//), [Shu Yang](https://shuyang.wordpress.ncsu.edu/), and [Leonard A. Stefanski](https://www4.stat.ncsu.edu/~stefansk/). Before joining the Ph.D. program, I earned a B.S. in Life Sciences (thesis advisor: Professor [Sung Wook Chi](https://clip.mystrikingly.com/#professor)) and a B.Econ. in Statistics from Korea University, followed by an M.S. in Statistics from Seoul National University (advisor: Professor [Byeong Uk Park](https://sites.google.com/view/bupark/)).

My transition into statistics grew out of early experiences in ecology, including work at the Biodiversity and Ecology Lab and assessing endangered Grylloblattidae (icebugs or rock-crawlers) species for the IUCN Red List. Through this work, I recognized both the rapid shift in modern biology toward high-throughput molecular data and the central role that mathematical and statistical frameworks play in describing ecological and genetic phenomena. Seeking a deeper understanding of molecular data, I studied transcriptome-scale RNA interference at the [Molecular and Computational Genomics Lab](https://clip.mystrikingly.com/). Driven by a desire for rigorous theoretical statistics and a focus on causal mechanisms rather than associations, I transitioned into graduate training in statistics.

My research focuses on causal inference, specifically addressing unmeasured confounding and principal stratification in observational studies, as well as high-dimensional methods. Grounded in my early background in ecology and genomics, I am interested in bringing methodological insights to problems in genomics and evolutionary biology.
